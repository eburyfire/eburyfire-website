# False alarms: the boring fix

Between April 2023 and March 2024, London Fire Brigade attended around 52,000 false alarms from automatic fire alarms. That's about 142 a day ([London Fire Brigade](https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/about-us/services-and-facilities/false-alarms-and-lift-calls/responding-to-fire-alarms/)).

Each one empties a building, interrupts a business and makes the next alarm a little easier to ignore. Most false alarms have a cause you can find and a fix you can book.

## Name the cause first

"We get a lot of false alarms" doesn't tell anyone what to fix. Most fall into one of four groups:

- **Something in the air:** cooking fumes, steam, dust, aerosols, contractors cutting or sanding.
- **Something wrong with the kit:** a dirty or ageing detector, insects inside, a faulty device.
- **Someone pressing a call point** when there's no fire.
- **Something wrong with the system:** a wiring fault, a panel fault or a setting that was never right.

The log book usually tells you which. Same device, same time of day, same activity nearby: that's your pattern.

## Fixes on site

- **Kitchens get heat detectors.** A smoke detector by the toaster will go off. Fit the right detector and the toast can stay.
- **Brief contractors before they start.** Dust covers on, the system isolated properly, and back on when they finish.
- **Protect call points that get knocked.** Covers and better positions stop most accidental presses.
- **Replace tired detectors.** Over the years dust and insects build up inside and make them jumpy, and a new detector fixes it.

## Fixes in the system

Depending on the design, a competent person can:

- use multi-sensor detectors, which look for more than one sign of fire before raising the alarm
- set some detectors to need a second signal before they alarm
- add a short, controlled delay so staff can check before a full evacuation, where the fire strategy allows it

These are design changes, so they need someone competent to make and record them.

## When it happens

1. Follow your evacuation procedure.
2. Write it in the log book before you reset: time, zone, device and what you think caused it.
3. Don't reset if the brigade is on the way. They'll want to see what triggered it.
4. Tell your monitoring centre if you have one.

If the same device keeps going off, there's a reason, and another reset won't find it. Get it looked at.
