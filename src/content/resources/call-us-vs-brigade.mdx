# Fire, fault or false alarm: who to call

**If you think the building is on fire, call 999. Always.** Get everyone out, call the brigade, and deal with the panel afterwards.

Most of the time, though, the panel is telling you something that isn't a fire. This guide is for those moments.

## Read the panel

A fire alarm panel has three states worth knowing:

- **Normal:** a green light and silence. All is well.
- **Fault:** a yellow light and a beeping buzzer. Something inside the system has gone wrong, and it may not detect a fire until it's fixed. Pressing reset won't fix it.
- **Fire:** a red light, sounders going and a zone shown. Treat it as real until you know otherwise.

Even when you're sure it's toast, follow your evacuation procedure. If there is any doubt at all, call 999.

## In London, check whether you need to call 999 yourself

London Fire Brigade no longer responds to automatic fire alarms from some non-residential buildings between 7am and 8.30pm unless someone calls 999. Residential buildings and schools still get a response. [Check whether your building is affected](https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/safety/the-workplace/automatic-fire-alarms/), and make sure your fire procedure says who makes the call.

## Call us for

- a fault on the panel that won't clear
- a damaged or missing detector, sounder or call point
- false alarms that keep coming from the same place
- calls in the night from your monitoring centre that you want looked into
- changes to the system before building work starts
- moving your fire alarm maintenance over to us

Your maintenance contract says how call-outs work and how to report a fault.

## Call 999 for

- any sign of fire, smoke or unusual heat
- sounders going and nobody knows why
- an evacuation in progress before the cause is confirmed
- anyone vulnerable who can't be accounted for

If the alarm has gone off for real, don't reset the panel before the brigade has been. They'll want to see what triggered it.

## If your alarm is monitored

If your system sends signals to an alarm receiving centre, find out what they do with them. Some pass every signal to the fire service. Others call you first to check. Keep their number by the panel, so you can tell them quickly when an alarm turns out to be false.
