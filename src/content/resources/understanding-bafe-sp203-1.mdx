# Understanding BAFE SP203-1: what it means for your building

BAFE SP203-1 is a well-known third-party certification scheme for fire detection and alarm contractors in the UK. If you've asked a contractor "are you BAFE certified?", this is usually what they mean. But the scheme covers a specific scope, and the badge alone doesn't answer every question a building owner needs to ask.

## What SP203-1 covers

SP203-1 is a scheme owned by BAFE, the Fire Safety Register, and delivered through UKAS-accredited certification bodies. It is a modular scheme covering four areas of work on fire detection and alarm systems to BS 5839-1: design, installation, commissioning and handover, and maintenance.

A certified company has been audited against the scheme's requirements and is re-audited periodically. The certificate covers the company for the modules it has been audited against — a maintenance-only certification doesn't mean the same contractor can design a new system.

## What it doesn't cover

SP203-1 is fire alarm specific. It doesn't cover:

- Gas suppression (BS EN 15004), which is a separate competence
- Emergency lighting (BS 5266)
- Voice alarm (BS 5839-8) — though related, this is a separate competence
- Watermist or pre-action systems (BS 8489)

If your building needs more than fire alarm, ask each contractor what they're certified for on each system. "BAFE" alone is not a blanket competence claim.

## What auditors and insurers look for

Fire Risk Assessors and building insurers often ask for evidence that maintenance is being carried out by a third-party certified contractor. BAFE SP203-1 is a common route for fire detection and alarm work. Check your own insurer's and assessor's requirements.

The evidence pack should include:

1. A current SP203-1 certificate covering the relevant module (for ongoing service contracts, the maintenance module)
2. The service certificate for each visit, signed by a competent engineer
3. The Log Book showing day-to-day events and corrective actions

A maintenance contractor who can't produce the first item should be asked how they evidence competence instead.

## Picking a contractor: the right questions

- Are you BAFE SP203-1 certified, and for which modules?
- Can I see your current certificate and the schedule of work it covers?
- Who carries out the visits, and whose name is on the certificate?
- What evidence pack do I get after each visit?

The certificate alone is necessary but not sufficient. The right contractor will be able to answer all four clearly.
