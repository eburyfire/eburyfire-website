# Is the fire alarm my job?

Short answer: if you run a business from the premises, or you control a building's shared parts, it probably is. At least partly.

In England and Wales, the rules come from the Regulatory Reform (Fire Safety) Order 2005, usually called the Fire Safety Order. It doesn't say "the landlord" or "the tenant". It says the "responsible person", plus anyone else who has control of the premises. Here's what that means in practice.

## The responsible person

Under article 3, the responsible person is:

- **in a workplace,** the employer, if the workplace is to any extent under their control
- **anywhere else,** the person who controls the premises in connection with a business or other undertaking (profit or not), or the owner, where the person in control doesn't

Run a dental practice, a shop or a gym? For your own premises, that's almost certainly you.

## Everyone else with control

Article 5 goes further. The same duties apply to anyone else who has control of the premises, to the extent of what they control. And if a lease or contract makes someone responsible for maintaining the building, or anything in it, they count as having control to that extent.

That's why the shared fire alarm in a parade with flats above is so often the landlord's, or the managing agent's. They control the common parts, and the lease usually says who maintains what.

## What "maintained" means

Article 17 says the fire safety equipment must be "subject to a suitable system of maintenance" and kept "in an efficient state, in efficient working order and in good repair" ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2005/1541/article/17)).

For a fire alarm, the accepted way to show that is the British Standard, BS 5839-1. It puts servicing at approximately every six months, alongside the weekly call point test and a log book that records both.

## Shared buildings: who does what

In a mixed building, duties often split:

- the landlord or agent looks after the shared alarm in the common parts
- each tenant looks after anything in their own unit, and their own weekly test
- everyone keeps the others informed

Where it gets messy is the bit everyone assumes someone else is handling. Read the lease. If it's unclear, agree it in writing.

## The boring fix

Find out what you control. Get the system serviced to BS 5839-1. Keep the certificate somewhere you can find it, and the log book by the panel.
