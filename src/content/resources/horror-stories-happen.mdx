# Horror stories happen: what fire services found in 8,932 shops

In the year to March 2026, fire and rescue services in England carried out 50,195 fire safety audits. Shops were audited more than any other type of premises: 8,932 of them. Only 58% of those shops were found satisfactory ([gov.uk, published 2 September 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/fire-prevention-and-protection-england-year-ending-march-2026/fire-prevention-and-protection-statistics-england-april-2025-to-march-2026)).

One caveat: inspectors focus on premises they think are higher risk, so this isn't a picture of every shop. But it's a lot of shops.

The same year, fire services issued 1,224 prohibition notices, which stop people using all or part of a building. That's up 27% on the year before.

## Three real cases

**A hotel fined £75,000.** Inspectors found a faulty fire alarm at a hotel in Wellingborough and served an enforcement notice to fix it. When they came back months later, the work hadn't been done. The company was fined £75,000, plus costs and a victim surcharge ([Northamptonshire Fire and Rescue Service, November 2025](https://www.northantsfire.gov.uk/2025/11/05/prosecution-results-in-wellingborough-hotel-being-fined-75k-for-fire-safety-failures/)).

**Care home directors ordered to pay £124,455.** Across four care homes in Bexhill-on-Sea, inspectors found defective or missing fire alarm systems, damaged fire doors and out-of-date fire risk assessments. The two directors pleaded guilty to 12 offences and were sentenced in July 2024 ([FIA report](https://www.fia.uk.com/news/care-home-directors-fined-125-000-for-fire-safety-breaches-across-four-premises.html)).

**A restaurant with no fire detection at all.** In Northampton, staff were found sleeping in a loft above a restaurant with no fire detection. The fire service banned the loft from use and the owner was prosecuted ([FIA report](https://www.fia.uk.com/news/northampton-restaurant-owner-fined-for-severe-fire-safety-breaches.html)).

## What usually goes wrong

It's rarely dramatic. It's the boring stuff left undone:

- a fault light that's been on so long nobody sees it
- servicing that slipped, then slipped again
- a certificate nobody can find
- a weekly test nobody has written down since the last manager left
- a notice from the fire service that sat in a pile

## The boring fix

Get the system serviced to BS 5839-1. Keep the certificate where you can find it. Test a call point every week and write it down. When a fault light comes on, get it fixed.
