# What a fire alarm service visit should cover

Servicing is the most thorough check your fire alarm system gets. BS 5839-1:2025 sets servicing at approximately every six months, but the certificate at the end is only as good as the work behind it. Here is what a properly scoped service visit covers, in order.

## Before the visit

Before arriving on site, the engineer should review:

- The previous service certificates
- The Log Book entries since the last visit, especially faults and false alarms
- The site asset register
- Any modifications notified since the last visit
- Outstanding actions or deferred works

If anything in the file looks wrong, it should be raised with you before the visit, not after.

## On arrival

The engineer checks in with the Responsible Person, confirms which areas are out of bounds, and isolates the system from any monitored receiving centre to avoid sending false alarm signals during testing.

## Visual inspection

The engineer walks every room covered by the system and inspects every device, looking for:

- Devices that have been painted over, decorated, or obstructed
- New ceiling layouts, partitions, or cable trays that affect coverage
- Damage to devices, sounders, or call points
- Cables that have been pulled, kinked, or removed
- Areas that were not in the design but are now occupied differently

Anything found should be documented with its location, and either rectified during the visit or quoted as remedial work.

## Testing detectors

Each type of detector is tested with a method suited to it:

- **Smoke detectors** with a calibrated test aerosol that triggers the sensing element
- **Heat detectors** with a controlled heat source
- **CO detectors** with calibrated test gas
- **Multi-sensor detectors** through each sensing element separately
- **Beam detectors** by interrupting the beam path under controlled conditions

Each device's pass or fail should be logged against its serial number and address. A device that fails goes on the remedials list with its location and proposed replacement type.

## Testing call points

Manual call points are tested with a test key, not a hammer. The engineer verifies that the panel sees the activation and resets correctly afterwards.

## Sounders and beacons

The evacuation signal is tested. Sound pressure level is measured at representative points, and the engineer checks:

- That all sounders activate
- That beacons fire in synchronisation
- That cause-and-effect rules trigger the right zones

Where there are dead zones or inaudible areas, those go to remedials.

## Panel and power supply

The panel itself is checked: firmware version, event log, internal time and date, and battery condition under load. Standby batteries are load-tested, or replaced if they're at end of life.

## Interfaces

If the system controls anything else (magnetic door retainers, air handling, gas suppression release, lift homing), each interface should be tested for correct cause-and-effect. A wrong cause-and-effect can be worse than no system at all.

## Records and handover

At the end of the visit, expect:

- Updated Log Book entries for every action taken
- An inspection and servicing certificate, signed
- An asset register update with any device replacements
- A separate remedials quote, with each line item priced, if work is needed
- Reset of the monitored signal and confirmation back to the ARC

If your last service produced a single-page certificate and no remedials list, ask your contractor what they actually tested. The work and the paperwork should match.
