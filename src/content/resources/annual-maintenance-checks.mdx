# What a boring service visit looks like

The best fire alarm service visit is the one you barely notice. It's booked ahead, done properly, explained before it ends and filed where you can find it. Here's what "done properly" means, so you can tell a thorough visit from a quick walk round with a clipboard.

BS 5839-1:2025 puts fire alarm servicing at approximately every six months. The certificate at the end is only worth what was tested to earn it.

## Before anyone arrives

The visit starts with the paperwork. Your provider should read:

- the last service certificates
- the log book since the last visit, especially faults and false alarms
- the asset register for the building
- any changes made to the system since last time
- anything left outstanding

If something in your records looks wrong, you should hear about it before the visit.

## On arrival

The engineer checks in with whoever is responsible on site, agrees which areas are off limits, and tells the monitoring centre (if you have one) that testing is about to start. Nobody wants a fire engine for a test.

## A look round

Every room the system covers gets a look, and every device gets checked for:

- paint, decorations or stock in the way
- new partitions, ceilings or cable trays that change what it can detect
- damage to detectors, sounders or call points
- rooms now used for something the design never covered

Anything found is written down with its location, then fixed on the day or quoted separately.

## Testing, device by device

Each kind of detector has its own test: smoke detectors with a test aerosol, heat detectors with a heat source, carbon monoxide detectors with test gas, beam detectors by blocking the beam, and multi-sensor detectors through each sensor in turn. Every result, pass or fail, goes on record against the device.

Call points are tested with a key, not a hammer. The panel should register each one and reset cleanly.

## Sounders, beacons and the panel

Sounders need to be heard everywhere they should be, so the sound level gets measured. Beacons should flash together. If the alarm switches anything else, such as door holders, ventilation or lifts, each of those gets tested too.

At the panel, the engineer checks the event log, the clock and the standby batteries under load. Batteries near the end of their life are replaced or quoted.

## What you're left with

- log book entries for everything that was done
- a signed service certificate
- an updated asset register if anything was replaced
- a separate quote, line by line, if anything needs fixing
- confirmation that the monitoring centre knows testing has finished
